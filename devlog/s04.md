# s04: the café is playable

**Feature:** the first real game loop.
1. Customers queue; the one at the front gets an **order bubble** (a random real recipe, sometimes customised: oat/almond milk, extra shot, cinnamon or sprinkles).
2. Behind the bar you make it by **clicking 3D stations**: cups → espresso machine (one shot per click) → milk jug (steamed / thick foam / microfoam) → drawers (milk type, hot water, chocolate, toppings) → whipped-cream can.
3. The cup on the counter **fills layer by layer** (espresso, chocolate, water, milk, foam), with pour streams and falling sprinkles.
4. Ring the **bell** to serve: scored against the order (−25% per wrong detail), coins = price × accuracy + a 2-coin tip for perfect, then the customer leaves (dequeue) and the next one steps up.

**Lines worth showing** (`src/world/Bar.tsx`): clicking a 3D object.
```tsx
// R3F casts a ray from the mouse into the scene and calls onClick on the first mesh it hits
<mesh name="station:machine" visible={false} onClick={() => shop.shot()}>
  <boxGeometry args={[1.4, 1.3, 1.0]} />
</mesh>
```
Concept: **raycasting**. Your 2D click becomes a 3D ray, and the first thing it hits wins.

**What broke:**
1. The cup sat on the far side of the counter and was tiny: you couldn't see it fill. Moved it front-and-centre, 1.7× bigger, and tilted the barista camera to look *down* into it.
2. The serve bell was behind the pastry-case glass at the new angle, so the ray hit the glass first (raycasting picks the *first* hit!). Moved the bell next to the cup.
3. 🪙 doesn't exist in the Windows 10 emoji font (empty box), so it's 💰 now.

**Test:** a headless play-test clicks the real 3D stations by projecting each station to screen coordinates; it scored 100% on cappuccino, oat cappuccino and americano.
